## Full script (~2:45)

Have you ever stared at a loading spinner and wondered why an AI answer still takes so long—even when the question feels simple?

Here is the hidden issue. Modern AI applications do not behave like one short chat. They are closer to a **busy restaurant**. Many small tasks arrive at once, and a surprising amount of work repeats the same long setup—like rereading the same recipe before every single dish.

My slide uses a kitchen metaphor on purpose. On the left, orders pile up, one stressed cook handles everything in a line, and time slips away. That is the bottleneck: **we redo the same preparation again and again**.

On the right, the kitchen becomes organized. Similar orders are grouped, common ingredients are prepped once and shared, and multiple cooks coordinate. In research language, this is the intuition behind **efficient execution for structured language-model programs**: you organize multi-step AI workflows so the system can **reuse safe, shared work** instead of recomputing it.

My research aim is practical: **make these AI programs faster and cheaper to run at scale**, especially when tasks share long instructions or templates. The significance is direct—**faster responses for users and lower compute bills for labs and companies**—without requiring a brand-new model every time.

So the story is simple. When we stop treating every AI request like a totally separate emergency, and start running the kitchen with coordination and shared prep, the whole service speeds up.

Thank you.
